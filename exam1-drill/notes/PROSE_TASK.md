# Guides, Reference and Tell apart: teach the concept, cut the quote dump

Edit ONLY `src/guide.js`, `src/reference.js`, `src/tell.js` (JS template strings: no backticks, no `${`). Keep every `<!--FIG:…-->`, `<!--IMG:…-->`, `<!--GRAPH:…-->` marker and every `data-q` attribute. Do not touch diagrams.js, app.js, shell.html or the banks.

The learner's verdict: the pages are quote dumps. "WHAT IT IS" lists of 10 bullets each carrying a 40-word transcript quote; a Distinctions table whose cells are paragraphs; nothing that lets a reader look at a figure and say what was introduced. Rewrite so each page teaches the concept first and uses his words only where the wording is the thing to remember.

## Rules for every section
1. **What it is**: at most 5 bullets, each ≤ 25 words, stating the mechanism or rule. One quote per section at most, ≤ 15 words, only if it is a phrase he will reuse on the exam (e.g. "helping or making life more difficult", "the smaller the Kd, the greater the affinity", "do the opposite"). Cites stay as `<small>…</small>` at the end of the bullet, shortened to the page/slide number.
2. **How to read it on a curve / figure**: for each figure keep the IMG marker and replace the quote bullets with a four-cell reading strip in this exact markup so it renders as a table:
   `<table class="readstrip"><tr><th>Shift</th><th>Baseline</th><th>Emax</th><th>Symmetry</th><th>Answer</th></tr><tr><td>right</td><td>0, unchanged</td><td>same</td><td>equal steps</td><td><b>competitive antagonist</b></td></tr></table>` followed by ONE line in prose that says what the student would get wrong and why the strip settles it. Use "—" in a cell when the figure cannot show it (single shift → symmetry "—"; baseline already 0 → "0, cannot tell").
3. **Distinctions**: three columns: `Looks the same | The one thing to look at | Answer`. Every cell ≤ 15 words. One row per confusable pair.
4. **How he asks it**: keep the poll boxes but cut each to: the stem in his words, the key, one line on why. Drop "Settled in class" quotes longer than 15 words.
5. **Traps**: ≤ 5 bullets, ≤ 20 words each, each naming the wrong move and the right one.
6. Guide 10 ("How to tackle a curve question") becomes the first thing a reader needs: (a) a numbered procedure of five lines (point of reference → helping or hurting? → shift → baseline → Emax → symmetry if several shifts), (b) a decision table `Observation | Rules out | Leaves` with one row per observation (shift left; shift right; baseline up; baseline down to 0; baseline unchanged at 0; Emax down; Emax up; equal steps; unequal steps), (c) the figure `<!--FIG:outcomes-->` placed after the procedure (it will exist: a grid of before/after mini plots), (d) the review-page figures as worked examples with the reading strip of rule 2. Keep the "Knowing the drug on the list is a shortcut" and "draw it out" points as one bullet each.
7. Reference: the same three-column form for its distinctions table (the one in the Day 6 section); every other table keeps its columns but no cell over 25 words; the "What you are looking up" subtitle stays.
8. Tell apart: the Day tables are fine in shape; cut any cell over 25 words to the rule; keep `data-q`.
9. **Anchors**: give every `<h3>` in the three files an `id`: guides `id="guide-N"` (N = the guide number, 1–10), Reference `id="ref-N"` for the numbered sections and `id="ref-classes"`, `id="ref-druglist"`, `id="ref-three"` for the first three unnumbered ones, Tell apart `id="tell-shifts"`, `id="tell-indirect"`, `id="tell-enhance"`, `id="tell-gpcr"`, `id="tell-day1"` … `id="tell-day6"`, `id="tell-druglist"`. Keep heading text short (≤ 8 words after the number).
10. Literal language (no metaphors beyond his own quoted phrase), no sentence about the page or the writing, every fact kept traceable to its existing cite; do not add facts or numbers that are not already on the page or in notes/L0X.md.
