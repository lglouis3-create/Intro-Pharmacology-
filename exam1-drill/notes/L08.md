## Conflicts / uncertain
Resolved or updated by the 10/5 audio:
- **Physostigmine for atropine poisoning.** Slide 39: "Drug of choice for treating poisoning due to anti-muscarinic agents (e.g., atropine)". Katzung 16e Ch. 8 (Adverse Effects, p. 10/14): "Poison control experts discourage the use of physostigmine ... to reverse the effects of atropine overdose because symptomatic management is more effective and less dangerous." 10/5 audio agrees with the slide: "Pfizostigamine typically is gonna be used for as an antidote if somebody has this, uh, you know, overdose on, say, atropine". Keyed from slide and lecture; Katzung kept in the `note` and `reading` on L08-031.
- **Enzyme name.** He again says "pseudocholinesterase" and treats the names as one: "let's talk about the pseudocholesterase inhibitors, uh, also known as cholesterrase enzyme inhibitors. There's different variations of that name. ... So these drugs are gonna bind and inhibit the acetylcholinesterase enzyme". Questions keep acetylcholinesterase (slides); no question separates the two names.
- **Muscle order (slide 27).** Settled: for curare-like drugs he taught it as the order of paralysis: "You're gonna start with, you know, the, in this case, the eyes, the jaws, and the, the larynx, and then you go to the extremities, and eventually you can stop the diaphragm from being able to contract". New question L08-047. For succinylcholine he gave a different order ("Typically, you start with your large limbs, legs and arms, and then you're going to the face, and then eventually, if you give too much, it can affect the diaphragm"); that order is not on a slide and is only in the L08-047 `note`.
- **Mivacurium and tubocurarine.** Tubocurarine: "The first one that we designed was called tubercurarine, but it had a lot of side effects, so we don't use that anymore as we used to." Mivacurium was not named on 10/5. Only class membership is tested (L08-022, L08-040).
- **Slide 38 "Question?".** No question was asked about edrophonium; he explained the handwritten "Vol of Dist" (very small volume of distribution, water soluble, very quick on and off) and said he is not too worried about edrophonium for the exam. L08-029 was rewritten to the question he did ask (physostigmine or neostigmine for myasthenia gravis).
- **Dose–response curves (slides 29, 36).** He said Exam 2 will have no dose–response curves; L08-023 and L08-027 (formerly `figure` questions) now ask the same facts in words; the slide's curve shift is kept in each `note`.
- **Curare-like side effects (slides 30, 32).** He taught them (pancuronium tachycardia, atracurium histamine release, rocuronium or vecuronium as his pick) but limited the exam to the shared mechanism; L08-024 and L08-025 were rewritten, the specifics stay in their teach text.
- **Galantamine (slide 40).** Not needed for the exam (only rivastigmine and donepezil); removed from L08-033. The student drug list still lists galantamine.
- **Edrophonium (slide 38).** Not a concern for the exam; it remains only as a distractor (diagnosis only) in L08-022, L08-029, L08-031 and L08-048. The student drug list still lists edrophonium.
