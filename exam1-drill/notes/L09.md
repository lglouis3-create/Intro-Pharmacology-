## Conflicts / uncertain
- **Atropine dose in organophosphate poisoning**: slide 20 "2-5 mg every 10-15 min"; Katzung Ch. 8 "1–2 mg of atropine sulfate ... intravenously every 5–15 minutes". Keyed to the slide (L09-029 note).
- **Tiotropium receptors**: slide 28 "M1 and M3"; Katzung Table 8–1 lists tiotropium among M3 antagonists. The drug list agrees with the slide (M1 & M3) and adds "Heart rate not affected because it does not target M2" (not on the slide; not used). Keyed to the slide (L09-034 note).
- **Tolterodine and trospium selectivity**: slide 30 puts tolterodine (with fesoterodine) under M1 & M3 and trospium in the M3 group with the fewest CNS effects; Katzung calls tolterodine M2- and M3-selective, fesoterodine M3-selective, and trospium nonselective and comparable to oxybutynin. Keyed to the slide (L09-038 note).
- **Bladder drugs "non-selective" vs "more selective toward M3"**: slide 29 says both in the MOA line; the question uses both phrases as written.
- **Drug list vs slide 30**: the drug list says solifenacin, darifenacin and trospium are "Most selective for M3 → least likely to cause anti-DUMBbELSS"; slide 30 ranks them least for CNS effects and says all have the potential to cause anti-DUMBBELSS, with xerostomia and constipation (M3) in all muscarinic antagonists. Slide wins (L09-039 note).
- **β receptor on the heart cell (slides 7–8)**: the M2 figure labels the NE receptor "β2" (Gαs). Exam 1 drug-list material pairs the heart with β1 (metoprolol). L09-010 avoids the subtype.
- **M1 location**: slide 5 text says "Enteric Nervous System and Neuron Ganglia" while the arrow and the slide 3/10 diagrams put M1 on the brain (Alzheimer disease, CNS emesis). Both are stated; questions use the brain/Alzheimer and CNS points and the slide 5 wording only in teach.
- **Choline esters**: slide 4 divides direct-acting drugs into alkaloids and choline esters, and slide 11 boxes "chol" in each choline drug name, but no slide labels acetylcholine, methacholine, carbachol or bethanechol individually as choline esters. Questions call only pilocarpine an alkaloid (slide 14) and do not assert the ester label for the others.
- **DUMBBELSS letters**: slide 13 prints the letters beside the table rows but never spells the words; the last S has no row of its own besides cognition. Questions use the table's row wording, not letter expansions.
- **Cevimeline**: only on slide 11 (with pilocarpine); "Radiation" is written beside the pilocarpine/cevimeline line without explanation. Not tested beyond the listed uses.
- **Endothelial NO pathway**: slides give only "M3 mediated release of Nitric Oxide" / "Vasodilation (via Nitric Oxide)". NOS → NO → sGC → cGMP is not on these slides (it belongs to the 10/7 NOS lecture), so it is not tested here.

## Objectives
Objectives
- Describe the types and distribution of muscarinic receptors
- Define muscarinic receptor's agonist and antagonists
- Identify receptor-linked second messenger systems and the corresponding effectors responses
- Distinguish the role of receptor transducers such as guanosine triphosphate binding proteins (G proteins) involved in the Muscarinic signaling pathway
