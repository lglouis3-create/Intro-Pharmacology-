## Conflicts and transcription points
- **Three vs four target areas.** Slide p. 10 title "Three Target Areas", three bullets. She: "I forgot to change my top. My title up here, but change this from 3 to 4 target areas", adding immune system targets (HLA). Keyed on four (L17-018, L17-024).
- **Who defined the terms.** She first said "if I recall, these are World Health Organization definitions", then "Oh, I'm sorry, this one is the American Association of Pharmaceutical Scientists ... pharmacogenomics focus group". The slide cites AAPS. Keyed on AAPS (L17-011).
- **Polymorphism cutoff wording.** Slide: "If present ≥ 1% of the population" (the text export shows ">" because the underline is lost; the rendered page shows ≥). She says "at least 1%", "greater than 1%", "more than 1%" and "1% or more". Keyed on at least 1% (L17-029, L17-030).
- **Haplotype vs linkage disequilibrium.** Slide p. 19: a haplotype is a set of alleles at multiple loci on one chromosome; a higher frequency than random chance is "linkage disequilibrium". She attributed the greater-than-chance frequency to the haplotype itself ("a haplotype has a statistical significance") and did not say "linkage disequilibrium". Both kept in L17-046; the term is tested from the slide under her "every slide with a term" rule.
- **Wild type.** Slide: "the version of a gene variation that is thought to be the most common". She: usually the most common, sometimes only the first understood; "most common" accepted on her test (L17-028).
- **Huntington's chorea.** "Huntington's chore", "Huntington's career"; she described it as a very specific early-onset dementia "caused by one genetic variant in one allele". Tested only as a named single-gene example (L17-042); the "one little snip" remark is not tested.
- **Hardy-Weinberg.** Slide: allele and genotype frequencies constant across generations unless specific influences are introduced. She: a statistical analysis of whether a variation is "Occurring because of evolutionary pressure or not", with a hypothetical stressed-class example. Both kept (L17-049, L17-050).
- **Renderings kept verbatim**: "Primaquin", "Prima Quinn" = primaquine; "Motosky", "Muussky" = Motulsky; "Frederick Vogel", "Friedrich Vogel" (slide "Fredrich Vogel") = Vogel; "antipyrene" = antipyrine; "McKinnon" = MacKinnon; "Wandrasek" = Wondrasek; "PGP P glacoprotein" = P-glycoprotein; "e-flux" = efflux; "Farm GKB" = PharmGKB; "University of San Diego" (PharmGKB is not tested by institution); "low side", "low sci", "low psi", "low si" = locus/loci; "world peas", "world peace" = wrinkled peas; "SIP 2C192", "CIP2C192", "STC 192", "2C192" = CYP2C19*2; "SAR 2", "SA 2" = star 2; "pharmacogenemics" = pharmacogenomics; "Hardy-Winberg" = Hardy-Weinberg; "hisstones" = histones; "MS" = MASH; "M should know" (a stray letter); "FSOP" = her school.
