## The question-reading method (how he wants a graph question tackled)
1. Find the reference curve (the drug alone). "This is our point of reference. This is what happens with nothing."
2. Ask "is it gonna help or make life more difficult?" — shift left (helping: full agonist, allosteric agonist, indirect antagonist that raises the signal, up-regulation) or shift right (competitive antagonist, inverse agonist, irreversible antagonist, allosteric antagonist).
3. Three questions: Is it shifting (ED50)? Is the baseline changing? Is the Emax changing? Then, when several shifts are drawn: are the shifts symmetrical?
   - Baseline down to 0 → inverse agonist. Baseline up → a second agonist (full or partial).
   - Emax down → irreversible antagonist (or allosteric antagonist on efficacy). Emax up (partial becomes full) → allosteric agonist on efficacy, PDE inhibitor, more receptors.
   - Symmetrical equal steps, no limit → competitive (reversible) antagonist. Unequal, saturating steps → allosteric.
4. With ONE right shift and no Emax change, competitive and inverse agonist (baseline 0) cannot be told apart, and an irreversible antagonist with spare receptors is still possible on a select-all; "I have to give you more information".
