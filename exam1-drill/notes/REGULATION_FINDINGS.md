## 2. "Why it matters" in his words

- **The whole point of the topic:** "you need to understand what is the effects of up-regulating or down-regulating receptors are going to have with drugs, because that's what we're here for." (t5 L32:c2175). "when we come back, we'll talk about the effects that it can have on the dose response curve" (t5 L32:c13500).
- **The rule the body follows:** "typically an antagonist will up regulate, an agonist is going to down regulate because our body is going to do the opposite to maintain that homeostasis. OK? So just think about it, what the drug function is ... and how your body would oppose that" (t5 L32:c3830). Repeated at the poll: "the body is going up regulate, uh uh, down regulate, right? Do the opposite." (t5 L32:c22895).
- **Tolerance (the down-regulation consequence):** slide 29 "Tolerance: The effects that follow continue exposure to the same concentration of drug is diminished" (p2 p29); "We have tolerance, we talked about that with patients who may use cocaine or opioids, drugs of addiction, right? Their body is going to suppress things." (t5 L32:c21570); cocaine: "instead of having one little sniff, you go to two sniffs ... It's gonna require more of it to be able to produce the response that it had before." (t5 L32:c16800–17400).
- **Opioids:** "If you are a smoker or you are addicted to opioids, your body is going to lower those receptor numbers because it's too much stimulation" (t5 L32:c7530); "Opioid Analgesics" listed under Tolerance (p2 p29).
- **Afrin:** "affin says in the back of the box? Only used for the more 2 to 3 days ... because afrin is an agonist, it's gonna do the opposite. It's gonna decrease the number of spare receptors." (t5 L32:c3300–3700); "If you're taking your Afrin, too much of an agonist, constricting your blood vessels in your nose, we're going down regulate that to counteract that stimulation" (t5 L32:c7655).
- **Stopping an agonist suddenly (Levophed):** "If you shut down that IV drip, bonk, they're gonna tank it out because there's just not enough receptors to do the job" (t5 L32:c18450).
- **Stopping a β-blocker (the up-regulation consequence):** "Do not go cold turkey. Because if you go cold turkey, you have lots of receptors that are being up regulated and no antagonists to block it, and that patient is going to be very likely to go into what is called a hypertensive crisis, which can be life threatening ... they have to wean themselves off, decrease the dose gradually" (t5 L32:c2691); slide 19 "Prolonged Use of Antagonist → Stepwise Tapering" (p2 p19); on 9/30: "It can cause upregulation. They can't go cold turkey. They got to wean themselves off." (t6 L32:c14607).
- **What the exam asks (his framing):**
  - Effect on the curve: "with the up regulation, I can make a full agonist more potent by shifting it to the left" (t5 L32:c4624); "that's gonna shift the dose response curve of norepinephrine to the right because it's gonna require more of it to produce equivalent responses" (t5 L32:c16300). Poll keyed D: "Decreasing the number of receptors may lead to decrease in the potency of an agonist" (t5 L32:c22800; notes/L05.md item 8); poll keyed E (all correct): "Increasing the number of receptors may increase the potency of a drug" (notes/L05.md item 9).
  - Which drug class each is analogous to: slide 20 "Analogous to the effects of irreversible acting antagonists" (p2 p20); "This is kind of analogous or similar to a person being in the presence of an irreversible antagonist. Why is that? Because my receptor population availability is being decreased" (t5 L32:c6998); and from the other direction on 9/30, the irreversible antagonist "will decrease the ability of agonists to get to the Emax because you're removing, you're down regulating the receptors chemically" (t6 L32:c2078). For up-regulation the slide ties it to spare receptors: "↑Spare Receptors · compensatory mechanism" (p2 p17); "we just have more spare receptors now to go around" (t5 L32:c2295). He never names a drug class that up-regulation is analogous to — the closest is "the more spare receptors I have, the more potent my agonist is going to be" (t5 L32:c4950). (Not in his words: the up-regulated curve looks like the curve with an allosteric/indirect "helper" that shifts left — textbook reasoning; notes/L06.md line 86 groups up-regulation with the "shift left" helpers.)

## 3. The chain from long-term down-regulation to the curve

Each link with his words; links he did not state are marked.

1. **Chronic agonist → the body removes receptors.** "an agonist is going to down regulate because our body is going to do the opposite to maintain that homeostasis" (t5 L32:c3830). "if I keep stimulating and stimulating and stimulating, then my body says ... we're just gonna have to get rid of the receptors and keep them low" (t5 L32:c18300). Slide 17: "Down-regulated: Follow chronic exposure of receptor to a stimuli → Agonist" (p2 p17).
2. **The receptor leaves the surface, so it cannot be bound.** "I have a removal of the receptors from the cell. And if the receptors are not in the cell wall, you can't bind to anything" (t5 L32:c7200). "we're going to engulf that receptor. We're going to bring it inside of the cell" (t5 L32:c17700). Slide 20: "Fewer receptors synthesized and available at cell surface" (p2 p20).
3. **Fewer receptors → the agonist needs more dose → potency down → shift RIGHT.** "as I lose receptors, now, what is that my drug has to do to be able to produce the same effect that did it before? I have to increase the concentration ... And that's gonna shift the dose response curve of norepinephrine to the right because it's gonna require more of it to produce equivalent responses" (t5 L32:c15900–16500). "now my agonist potency is going to decrease. Because now I have to give more agonists to be able to bind the same number of receptors" (t5 L8:c4300). Slide 28 handwriting: "less receptors available, ↓potency" (p2 p28). Poll key: "Decreasing the number of receptors may lead to decrease in the potency of an agonist" (notes/L05.md item 8).
4. **While spare receptors cover it, the Emax holds.** Stated for the irreversible-antagonist case, which he then calls analogous: "if I have more spare receptors, I'm going to get a lot more shifts before I see that drop on the efficacy for an irreversible antagonist ... it's going to take longer or a higher concentration of my irreversible before I go below the minimum number of receptors required to produce the max effect" (t5 L8:c3650). Slide 2: "Respond to a non-competitive or irreversible antagonist as if it were competitive (initially)" (p2 p2). The slide-26 figure itself shows the "NE after Down-reg" curve shifted right with the same height (notes/L05.md slide map row 26 and figure note line 177). **Not in his words for down-regulation specifically** (he never said "the Emax holds at first after down-regulation"; that step is carried by the analogy and the slide-26 picture — textbook reasoning).
5. **When receptors run short, the Emax falls.** "as I have to have to increase the dose more and more, I'm not only decreasing my affinity of my drug, but also I'm decreasing what? The ability to produce the max effect. I don't have enough receptors available to do that, right? So I'm shifting to the right. And I'm losing mine, just like I would with any irreversible antagonist. Less receptors available, I can't make the next response." (t5 L32:c20700–21200). "my irreversible antagonist is gonna drop much, much faster, the ability of my agonist to produce that max efficacy because I just have less receptors available" (t5 L8:c4500). Slide 3: "Emax drops faster in response to a non-competitive" (p2 p3); slide 28 handwriting "↓efficacy" (p2 p28). *Caution:* "decreasing my affinity" (t5 L32:c20800) contradicts his 9/24 statement "It's the same drug, same KD. Now it just have more receptors available, so the affinity and efficacy has not changed. What changes the tissue" (t3 L365:c49); treat "affinity" there as a slip for potency.
6. **The analogy to an irreversible antagonist.** Slide 20: "Analogous to the effects of irreversible acting antagonists" (p2 p20). "This is kind of analogous or similar to a person being in the presence of an irreversible antagonist. Why is that? Because my receptor population availability is being decreased, is being lowered" (t5 L32:c6998). "just like I would with any irreversible antagonist" (t5 L32:c21100). Reverse direction (9/30): the irreversible antagonist lowers Emax "because you're removing, you're down regulating the receptors chemically" (t6 L32:c2078).

**The mirror (up-regulation):**
- Antagonist → more receptors: "typically our body does this, what is called a compensatory mechanism, when we have chronic exposure to an antagonist or an inverse agonist" (t5 L32:c2295); slide 17 "Up-Regulated: Follow chronic reduction of receptor stimulation → Antagonist" (p2 p17).
- Agonist more potent, shift LEFT: "The more receptors I have, now I am going to require less drug to produce the same effect. So with the up regulation, I can make a full agonist more potent by shifting it to the left" (t5 L32:c4437–4700); slide 18 "Increase the potency of a full agonist" (p2 p18).
- Partial agonist can reach full: "I can even make a drug who behaves like a partial agonist to become a full agonist because now I have enough receptors to bind and produce the full response" (t5 L32:c4700); slide 18 "Increase the potency and/or maximal response of partial" (p2 p18).
- Antagonist weaker: "the antagonist will be less potent because it now has more. Chairs to cover" (t5 L8:c3300); "more receptors, better for the agonist. Less receptors, better for the antagonist" (t5 L8:c4750); slide 2 "Tissue with a lot of receptors ... Be less sensitive to antagonist" (p2 p2).
- Consequence when the antagonist stops: quote #5 (hypertensive crisis; taper).

## 5. Proposed 3-bullet take-aways (≤ 20 words each)

**Rapid desensitization (F3['desens-rapid'] / '-anim')**
- "GRK is phosphorylating ... which allows the beta arrestin to come and bind": the stop plug. (t5 L32:c11600)
- "Like a band-aid": milliseconds, reversible; the receptor stays in the membrane until the agonist comes off. (t5 L32:c8589, c11750)
- Exam: cAMP is "the second messenger"; short-term regulation does NOT relocate receptors into the cell. (t5 L32:c9150, c22950)

**Long-term down-regulation (F3['desens-long'] / '-anim')**
- "If it's not coming off": β-arrestin to coated pits, endocytosis, then recycle or lysosome. "That takes time." (t5 L32:c17500, c8700)
- Fewer receptors: the agonist must "increase the concentration", so its curve shifts RIGHT, "like any irreversible antagonist". (t5 L32:c16300, c21100)
- Consequence: tolerance (opioids, Afrin, cocaine); stop the agonist and "they're gonna tank it out". (t5 L32:c21570, c18450)

**Up-regulation (F3['upreg'] / '-anim')**
- "An antagonist will up regulate ... our body is going to do the opposite": more spare receptors. (t5 L32:c3884)
- "The more spare receptors I have, the more potent my agonist": shift LEFT; a partial can reach full. (t5 L32:c4950, c4700)
- β-blocker: "Do not go cold turkey" — hypertensive crisis; wean off gradually. (t5 L32:c2691)

**Up vs down overview (F3['regulation'] / 'downreg')**
- Rule: antagonist → up-regulate; agonist → down-regulate; "think about ... how your body would oppose that". (t5 L32:c3830)
- More receptors: agonist more potent, antagonist less. Fewer: agonist less potent, Emax "drops much faster". (t5 L8:c4300–4800)
- Down-regulation is "analogous to the effects of irreversible acting antagonists": shift right, then Emax falls. (p2 p20; t5 L32:c6998)
